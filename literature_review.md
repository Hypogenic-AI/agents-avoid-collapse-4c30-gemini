## Literature Review

### Research Area Overview
The study of "model collapse" or "ecosystem collapse" in LLMs evaluates whether AI models degrade in quality, diversity, or factual accuracy when recursively trained on data generated by themselves or other AI models. Recent work has extended this from single-model recursive training to multi-agent ecosystems, investigating whether a diverse population of models can sustain a healthy information ecosystem or cooperative environment.

### Key Papers

#### Paper 1: AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data
- **Authors**: Ilia Shumailov, Zakhar Shumaylov, Yiren Zhao, Nicolas Papernot, Ross Anderson, Yarin Gal
- **Year**: 2024
- **Source**: arXiv (2305.17493)
- **Key Contribution**: Introduces the formal concept of "model collapse."
- **Methodology**: Evaluates VAEs, GMMs, and LLMs when trained on synthetic data generated by previous generations of the same models.
- **Datasets Used**: Standard text corpora (e.g., wikitext).
- **Results**: Discovered that tails of the original data distribution disappear, leading to a homogenized output that forgets rare events.
- **Code Available**: Yes (various community implementations and follow-ups).
- **Relevance to Our Research**: Demonstrates the baseline "collapse" scenario when a population effectively has size N=1 or lacks diversity.

#### Paper 2: Epistemic diversity across language models mitigates knowledge collapse
- **Authors**: Damian Hodel, Jevin D. West
- **Year**: 2025
- **Source**: arXiv (2407.08480)
- **Key Contribution**: Directly addresses the "minimum viable population" and diversity hypothesis.
- **Methodology**: Evaluates multiple distinct LLMs forming an ecosystem, rather than a single model feeding itself.
- **Datasets Used**: Various reasoning and QA datasets.
- **Results**: Increasing AI ecosystem diversity (distinct model architectures/training distributions) mitigates collapse. Models with different "epistemic" viewpoints act as a balancing force.
- **Code Available**: No official link found, but methodology is reproducible.
- **Relevance to Our Research**: Answers "Will such an ecosystem always collapse?" (No, diversity mitigates it) and provides a basis for "What counts as different enough?" (Epistemic diversity).

#### Paper 3: Cooperate or Collapse: Emergence of Sustainable Cooperation in a Society of LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Giorgio Piatti et al.
- **Year**: 2024
- **Source**: arXiv (2402.04259)
- **Key Contribution**: Looks at "collapse" from a resource management and cooperation perspective (GovSim).
- **Methodology**: Simulates societies of LLM agents managing a common pool resource.
- **Datasets Used**: GovSim simulated environment.
- **Results**: Without advanced reasoning or "Universalization," most LLM societies exhaust resources and collapse. Diversity and communication are key.
- **Code Available**: Yes.
- **Relevance to Our Research**: Provides an alternative view on "population collapse" related to agent interactions and cooperation rather than just data generation.

#### Paper 4: Model Collapse Does Not Mean What You Think
- **Authors**: Rylan Schaeffer et al.
- **Year**: 2025
- **Source**: arXiv (2408.06456)
- **Key Contribution**: Critiques the inevitability of model collapse.
- **Methodology**: Meta-analysis and rigorous assessment of model collapse literature.
- **Results**: Concludes that real-world conditions (where models are fine-tuned on a mix of human and AI data, rather than strictly AI data) make collapse highly avoidable.
- **Relevance to Our Research**: Suggests that even a small "population" might not collapse if anchored by continuous external reality (human data).

### Common Methodologies
- **Recursive Training Loops**: Training a model at generation $t$ on data from generation $t-1$.
- **Multi-Agent Resource Games**: Placing LLM agents in environments where their collective actions dictate ecosystem survival (e.g., GovSim).

### Standard Baselines
- **Single-Model Self-Training**: The simplest baseline where a model trains purely on its own outputs.
- **Zero-Shot LLM baselines**: Standard model performance before any synthetic recursive training.

### Evaluation Metrics
- **Perplexity and Cross-Entropy**: To measure language modeling quality over generations.
- **Distributional Shift**: Measuring the distance (e.g., Wasserstein distance) between the original data distribution and the generated distribution.
- **Survival Rate**: In multi-agent scenarios, the percentage of simulations that do not exhaust the common resource.

### Gaps and Opportunities
- **Gap 1**: Defining exact thresholds for "diversity." What specifically makes two models diverse enough to prevent collapse? (Architecture, pre-training data, or decoding strategy?).
- **Gap 2**: The interaction between data-driven model collapse and agent-driven cooperation collapse in a shared environment.

### Recommendations for Our Experiment
Based on the literature review, to answer "Is there a minimum viable population for an LLM information ecosystem such that it does not collapse?":
- **Recommended datasets**: Large text corpora (e.g., Cosmopedia, Wikitext) to simulate pre-training, and specific prompts to simulate multi-agent dialogue.
- **Recommended baselines**: A single homogeneous LLM recursively training on itself (expected to collapse rapidly).
- **Recommended metrics**: Distributional metrics (Self-BLEU, Vendi Score) for text diversity, and accuracy on downstream tasks over multiple generations.
- **Methodological considerations**: We should simulate an ecosystem of $N$ agents. We can vary $N$ (e.g., 1, 2, 5, 10) and vary the *diversity* of the agents (e.g., same model with different temperatures, different model families like Llama vs. Mistral vs. Qwen) to find the "minimum viable population" and define "different enough".
